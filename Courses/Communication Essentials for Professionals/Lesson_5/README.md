## 📘 Lesson Overview
In the workplace, **how you write** is often just as important as **what you write**.

This session introduced the foundations of business writing, its key purposes, and the most common professional documents used across organizations.

---

## 🧭 Purposes of Business Writing
Business writing primarily serves two essential goals:

- **Exchanging business-related information**
- **Documentation and archiving** for accountability, traceability, and future reference

### Common Communication Directions
Professional communication flows in multiple directions, most commonly:

- **Top-Down Communication**  
  - Delivering decisions  
  - Sharing updates and news  
  - Calling teams to action  

- **Bottom-Up Communication**  
  - Proposing ideas and improvements  
  - Explaining situations  
  - Justifying actions and decisions  

---

## 📄 Common Business Documents
Organizations rely on a variety of written documents, including:

- Emails
- Memoranda (internal communication)
- Business letters (external communication)
- Meeting agendas and minutes
- Reports and proposals
- Legal and financial documents
- Promotional and marketing materials

Each document has a **specific purpose, audience, and tone**.  
Using the wrong format often leads to misunderstanding and inefficiency.

---

## 🧠 Activity: Writing a Professional Report (Use Case)
Participants were given a realistic workplace scenario:

- An institute planned a reward trip for employees
- A meeting was held to define destination criteria:
  - Unique experience  
  - Relaxing environment  
  - Reasonable price  

### Task
Write a professional report that:
- Documents the discussion and final decision
- Applies clarity, logical structure, and objectivity
- Reflects professional standards of business writing

**Outcome:**  
The activity demonstrated how structured writing supports clear decision-making and organizational alignment.

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways
- Professional writing supports **decisions**, not just communication
- The document format must match the **purpose and audience**
- Proper documentation protects both individuals and organizations
