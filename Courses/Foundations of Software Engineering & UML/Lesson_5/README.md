## 🔹 Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

The SDLC provides the roadmap for building software in a structured way. Its core stages include:

1. **Requirements**: Understanding user and business needs.
2. **Design**: Creating the architecture and system blueprint.
3. **Development**: Converting design into actual code.
4. **Testing**: Ensuring the system works correctly and meets expectations.
5. **Deployment**: Making the software available for users.
6. **Maintenance**: Updating, fixing, and enhancing after release.

A clear SDLC reduces risk, improves planning, and aligns all team members around a shared vision.

---

## 🔹 What Is Software Requirements Engineering (SRE)?

SRE is the structured process of discovering, documenting, and maintaining requirements. It ensures we build the right system, not just build it well.

### Why SRE matters:

-   Most software failures come from unclear or changing requirements.
-   Poor requirements = rework, cost overruns, and unhappy customers.

---

## 🔹 Requirements Engineering Process Steps

### 1️⃣ Elicitation (Gathering Requirements)

Techniques include interviews, surveys, observation, brainstorming, workshops, and prototyping.

📌 **Example**: Interviewing a hotel receptionist to understand how booking currently works.

### 2️⃣ Specification (Documenting Requirements)

Good requirements follow the SMART model — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Testable.

📌 **Example**: "System shall send a confirmation email within 10 seconds."

### 3️⃣ Validation (Ensuring Correctness)

Techniques: reviews, walkthroughs, prototypes, test-case generation.

**Goal**: Ensure requirements are consistent, complete, and testable.

### 4️⃣ Management (Tracking & Updating)

Tools like Jira, Azure DevOps, and Notion help teams track changes, priorities, and dependencies.

---

## Key Takeaways

✅ Clear requirements are the foundation of successful software projects.  
✅ SDLC gives structure, while SRE ensures clarity and alignment.  
✅ Elicitation, specification, validation, and management form the backbone of requirements engineering.
